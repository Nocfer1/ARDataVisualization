#First Lab session
Once we had the idea of the AR project for displaying TEM data, we started structuring and separating the workload of the project. Our starting point was the information we were visualizing - taken from subsurface resistivity measurements that have been processed on specialist software used by geophysicists. The most basic data structure in this set is a layer, a measurement from a given depth and a given resistivity value. These layers are then grouped into a station, or a collection of readings taken from a single and specific physical location. Then these are again grouped further into a line, a series of readings taken from point A to point B. Then, finally, these are collected into a project, an overarching structure that can be made up of hundreds of lines, all originating from a similar physical location, for example in the confines of the same city.

We began by considering the architecture of the project. The main design choice that would drive the rest of our project's structure and usability was the model placement technology as marker-based or markerless. As we discussed the approach, we forwarded our concern to our supervisor, who gave us a better idea and understanding of the choice we should go with. We achieved a clearer picture of how a real use case would look like, which is markerless. By following this approach, on a real case the user could give use of the AR project whenever and wherever it wants. 

After finding the best model placement approach based on the likeliest use case of the application, we decided to work on an initial prototype. This would help us better understand the final goal for this solution and what could be improved in further iterations. The requirements for this prototype were reading the processed data from a '.csv' file format, using it for creating a GameObject representing each layer and positioning them on top of each other to create stations. Then, we displayed these in a straight line regardless of their real world coordinates.

Already this prototype gave us a lot of information for the next lab session. Each station being an individual GameObject, while good enough in small data sets, led to major stutters on larger projects. Discarding the coordinates of each station and choosing to display them in arbitrarily straight lines also led to loss of valuable information. These would all be issues to address in more mature versions of the software solution.
